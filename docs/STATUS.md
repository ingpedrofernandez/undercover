# Status

The claims pilot in `docs/PLAN.md` is built: all six phases are done.

The harness in `docs/PLAN_HARNESS.md` is built: all six phases are done. The code review in `docs/HUMAN_REVIEW_HARNESS.md` has been worked through: the agent is split into `api`, `tools`, `harness` and `work` modules, the backend into a router per area, the instructions state goals rather than steps, and `docs/CODE_STANDARDS.md` and `docs/PROCESS.md` now hold the standards and the review step that would have caught it.

Every agent run is traced in Langfuse.

Durable state in `docs/PLAN_DURABLE.md` is built: all six phases are done. The claim agent works out payouts with UnderCover's payout rules, each claim's work is a Langfuse trace of its own in the claim's session, and the backend's records live in Supabase. The backend treats a repeated decision or payment as done, an approval is decided once it's paid, and a handler's sign-off records who and when, moves the claim back to in progress for the agent to pay, and asks the agent's service on port 8002 to pick it up, which pays it within seconds. If the service can't be reached, the call is logged and the sign-off stands. The claim agent is a LangGraph graph whose every step is saved in Supabase: it stops at an approval the backend holds, and `uv run -m agent` carries a stopped or signed-off claim on from its last saved step.

A swarm of claim agents and a scoreboard in `docs/PLAN_SWARM.md` is built: all six phases are done, with one criterion of the live trial not met (below). A dispatcher with no model has replaced the queue agent: a handler starts a dispatch on the queue page with up to 25 claim agents at once, and evaluations on the Scoreboard. The agent runs on GPT-6 Luna with low reasoning effort, and every model call's tokens and cost and every claim's time are recorded with the dispatch or evaluation they happened in.

## Running it

- `npm run dev` starts the backend on port 8000 and the agent's service on port 8002, both with `.env`, so records and checkpoints are in Supabase, and the dashboard on http://localhost:5173.
- `npm run seed` rebuilds the records in Supabase from `data/`, with the held-out claims and the handlers' decisions from `data/evals/` in tables of their own, putting every claim back in the queue and emptying the scoreboard. `uv run -m backend.seed` does the same for SQLite at `undercover.db` or `UNDERCOVER_DB`.
- On the queue page, "Start a dispatch" works the queue with N claim agents at once, from 1 to 25. On the Scoreboard, "Start an evaluation" works copies of the evaluation set or the held-out set, whole or just its five tiny claims. Only one runs at a time. `uv run -m agent CLM-1001` carries that claim on if it's in progress, or starts it if it's waiting, and prints its line. All of them use the real model through OpenRouter, GPT-6 Luna with low reasoning effort, or the model in `MODEL_NAME` with none, and send their traces to the Langfuse project whose keys are in `.env`.

## Built

- `backend/`: Supabase's Postgres in the `undercover` schema when `DATABASE_URL` is set, and otherwise SQLite at `undercover.db` or the path in `UNDERCOVER_DB`; the backend reads no `.env` itself. `main.py` creates the app, with `health`, and includes a router for each area. `database.py` declares the tables with SQLAlchemy Core and builds the engine: on Postgres a pool of at most 4 connections, with `search_path=undercover`, and each request waits without a thread for one of four slots. `seed.py` drops and rebuilds our tables from `data/`, with `held_out_claims` and `handler_decisions` from `data/evals/`, on startup when there's no `claims` table or with `npm run seed`, and leaves the checkpointer's tables alone. `claims.py` has the queue and the work on each claim (`list_claims`, `get_claim`, `start_claim`, `record_step`, `list_steps`, `record_decision`, `pay_claim`, `sign_off_claim`, `record_failure`); the queue leaves out evaluations' copies and a copy is never signed off. `usage.py` records each model call's tokens and cost (`record_model_call`) and each piece of work's time (`record_work_time`), with its run or none. `runs.py` has dispatches and what every run does: `start_dispatch` asks the agent's service and writes nothing, `create_dispatch`, `get_latest_dispatch` with its claims, cost, time and a summary built from the records, `take_claims` (the queue's claims in progress and waiting, or an evaluation's copies, oldest submitted first, recorded on the run), `finish_run` and `stop_runs`. `evaluations.py` has `start_evaluation`, `create_evaluation`, which copies a set's claims with new ids, `list_evaluations` and `get_evaluation`, scored with the measures in `scoring.py`; `run_records.py` holds what both routers share. `agent_service.py` is the one client for the agent's service (`UNDERCOVER_AGENT`, or port 8002): it asks it to resume a claim after a sign-off, and to start dispatches and evaluations, passing on its 409 or a 503 when it isn't running. `records.py`, `payouts.py` with `payout_rules.py`, `settings.py`, `policies.py` and `documents.py` are as before; `get_instructions` serves only `claim`. A test checks that every operation follows the API rules.
- `agent/`, in modules that depend on each other in one direction (`api` <- `tools` <- `harness` <- `dispatcher` <- `work` <- `service`): `api.py` holds every call to the backend, and retries connecting for about 15 seconds so the service can start beside the backend. `tools.py` holds the claim agent's tools. `harness.py` holds the model (GPT-6 Luna, through `ChatDeepSeek`, which keeps the reasoning OpenRouter sends), the claim graph from durable state, whose `agent` node now also records each reply's tokens and cost, a pool of at most 4 connections per process with a `PostgresSaver` of its own for each claim graph, tracing, and `work_claim(claim_id, run_id)`, the one way into the claim agent: it carries a claim in progress on or starts a waiting one, records its time, settles a failure as before, and lets rate limits and timeouts through unsettled. `dispatcher.py` is the dispatcher graph, as in `labs/week2day4.ipynb`: `list_claims`, one `Send` per claim to `work_claim` with `max_concurrency` N, a `RetryPolicy` on rate limits and timeouts and an `error_handler` that leaves a claim for the next dispatch, then `list_retries` and `retry_claim` for the claims that failed once. `work.py` runs dispatches and evaluations from start to finish, clears every copy's saved progress after an evaluation, and marks runs left running as stopped. `service.py` starts dispatches and evaluations in the background, one at a time (a second start gets a 409), resumes a claim after a sign-off, skips claims it's resuming when a dispatch takes its claims, and marks runs left running as stopped when it starts. `__main__.py` works one claim.
- Tracing: `harness.tracing()` is the one Langfuse handler, which also puts the cost OpenRouter reports on each generation. Each piece of work on a claim is a trace of its own, `work-claim`, in the session `<claim id>:<thread id>`, tagged `run-<run id>` when it is part of a dispatch or evaluation. The tests keep traces in memory and never send them.
- `dashboard/`: the queue (`/`), with counts by state, the start form, the latest dispatch with each of its claims, its model cost and time and its summary, and a line per claim. A claim in full (`/claims/:claimId`), as before; a copy says which evaluation it belongs to and has no sign-off form. The Scoreboard (`/scoreboard`) with its start form and every evaluation's measures, and each evaluation (`/evaluations/:runId`) with the handler's decision beside the agent's and the misses marked. The claim agent's instructions (`/instructions`). Styled to `BRAND.md` in light and dark mode.
- `e2e/`: tests of the pages, the agent's steps on a claim page, a claim failing twice, a handler signing off a held payout, starting a dispatch and an evaluation from the dashboard, and a dispatch and an evaluation played out through the API. Playwright runs its own backend on port 8001, its own dashboard on port 5174 and a stand-in for the agent's service on port 8003, which records the claims it's asked to resume and the runs it's asked to start.
